Research notes, p. 1

An introduction

I'm a biotechnology graduate from Sambalpur, Odisha. My master's thesis at Sambalpur University asked whether an extract of Ailanthus excelsa could calm inflammation and oxidative stress. I prepared and standardised the extracts, ran DPPH and FRAP antioxidant assays, and carried out in vitro and in vivo anti-inflammatory studies.

I came to biotechnology through biochemistry at Centurion University. There I identified 84 WRKY transcription factor genes in the grass Dichanthelium oligosanthes, and used RT-PCR to follow odorant-binding protein expression in the rice moth Corcyra cephalonica.

The thread through all of it is the same question: what does a natural compound actually do inside a living system? I'm now looking for a PhD or research associate position where wet-lab work and computational analysis meet.

Case file 02, 2021 to 2022

Genome-wide analysis of WRKY transcription factors in Dichanthelium oligosanthes

Bachelor's thesis, Centurion University of Technology and Management

Question
Which WRKY transcription factor genes does this grass carry, and how are they related?
Finding
84 WRKY genes identified and named DoWRKY1 to DoWRKY84.

Method

  1. NCBI data retrieval
  2. BLAST and HMM profiling
  3. Alignment in MEGA, CLUSTAL W
  4. Phylogeny and motifs (MEME)
  5. Structure, GO and localisation
